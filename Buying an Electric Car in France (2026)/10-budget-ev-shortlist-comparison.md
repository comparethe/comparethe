# Budget/family EV shortlist — full comparison table

Everything from the budget/short-range track in one table: new and secondhand, price with and without incentive, safety, range, and rough running costs at 10,000km/year. Sorted by price, cheapest first.

## Why BYD Dolphin Surf wasn't on the list until now

It should have been — it's actually one of the strongest cars here. It didn't make earlier passes because the secondhand-market research kept flagging it as "too new to France to show real depreciation," which is true but isn't a reason to skip it as a *new*-car candidate — that was an oversight, not a deliberate exclusion. It's now in the table below, and once its bonus écologique eligibility resolves (see notes), it's genuinely competitive.

## Why some "modern" cars score so badly on safety

Worth explaining, since a 0-1 star result on a car sold today is genuinely surprising if you assume all modern cars are roughly equally safe — they're not, and the reasons are structural, not random:

- **Euro NCAP is voluntary and comparative, not a legal minimum.** Every car sold in the EU has to clear a legal "type approval" safety floor — but that floor is much lower than a Euro NCAP star rating. Manufacturers choose how much beyond the legal minimum to engineer for: extra structural reinforcement, more airbags, seatbelt pretensioners, pedestrian-friendly bonnet design, and AEB software tuning all cost real money per car, and a budget model can and does skip some of it to hit a price target. A low star rating on a legally-sold car isn't a mistake or a scandal — it's a manufacturer trading safety margin for price, which is exactly the trade this table is trying to make visible.
- **The rating is relative to the protocol at time of test, and the protocol gets stricter every few years.** The Renault Zoe is the clean proof: same car, no redesign, went from 5 stars to 0 stars purely because Euro NCAP's 2021 protocol raised the bar past what the car's original safety systems could clear. A 5-star rating from 2015 and a 5-star rating from 2025 are not the same bar — the newest tests in this table (Dolphin Surf, 2025) are the strictest of anything here.
- **This is not the same thing as "the car is dangerous to drive normally."** It means that *in a serious crash*, the measured outcomes for occupants — chest compression, head/neck loads, whether a child dummy's head contacts the interior — are worse than in a higher-rated car. That's a real, tested difference, not marketing spin, which is why it changed the recommendation here despite these being otherwise perfectly normal, legally-sold cars.

## Reading this table

- **Nissan Leaf remains the strongest single recommendation** — best safety-to-price ratio, real market, no open questions.
- **Three genuine 5-star options now exist**, not one: Leaf, Dolphin Surf, and e-208. Dolphin Surf has the best raw safety score of anything here but carries a real open question on the incentive; e-208 has the longest range and best resale but the smallest new-vs-used price gap.
- **Twingo E-Tech and ë-C3 are unresolved, not ruled out** — one has no safety rating yet, the other was wrongly marked unsafe in earlier research (now corrected) and is honestly just unrated for the actual car sold here.
- All of this is still first-cut, aggregator-sourced pricing and rough running-cost math — see [04-secondhand-market-survey.md](04-secondhand-market-survey.md) for the full caveats on data quality.
