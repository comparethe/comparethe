Price vs. mileage: a Pareto sketch for used EVs

Mileage as a rough stand-in for "quality" — lower is better (fresher car, more life left). Colour is make/model; marker shape is Tesla trim tier (circle = Propulsion/SR+, square = Grande Autonomie, triangle = Performance). Numbers by each point are the model year. The dashed line traces the Pareto frontier: points on it aren't beaten on both price and mileage by any other point on the chart.

Bottom-left is the "good" corner — cheaper and lower mileage. A point above/right of the frontier is dominated: something else on the chart is at least as good on both axes.

Update: the budget gap is filled — and the frontier collapses to two points. With real listings added for Spring, Leaf, e-208, ID.3, Atto 3, Zoe (plus a range-midpoint for the 500e), the price-vs-mileage frontier now runs through just BYD Dolphin Surf (100km, €19,990) and Dacia Spring (40,517km, €7,500) — nothing else in this table beats the Spring's price at any mileage, so everything past it is dominated on this axis alone, all the way out to 160,000km. That's the flaw in judging used EVs on price-and-mileage only: the Spring dominates this frontier the same way it dominates the new-car one, and it's ruled out elsewhere in this project on Euro NCAP safety (1★) — see 04 and the Track A table in index.md. A frontier that can't see safety, charging speed, or interior quality will keep recommending the Spring. Building a real quality-weighted frontier (not range/mileage as a stand-in) is now the top open item in plan.md.
Tesla points are band midpoints, not real listings: each is the middle of a sourced mileage range and price range (e.g. "2021 Propulsion, 50-90k km, €19-25k" → plotted at 70,000km / €22,000), not an actual advertised car. Treat the shape of the frontier as directional, not exact — see the "still needed" section in the secondhand survey for the plan to replace this with real listings.
